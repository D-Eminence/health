## Part B. Full notice

### Offline copies of your record

**Why**

Clinics often lose internet. Health ID can let a staff member keep an encrypted copy of some patient records on their own device, so work can go on. Changes made offline are sent when the connection returns. This is for clinic staff only.

**What is kept**

The copy holds only patients the staff member opened online with your permission. Staff can press "Keep available offline" for a patient, which saves your latest 3 visits, health profile and document list. The copy can include your name, Health ID number, visits, notes, vital signs, diagnoses, prescriptions, lab requests and released results, and the allergies and medicines you reported. Document files are never kept. Emergency records are never kept on the device.

**How long**

A clinic working without internet may keep seeing records it already opened for up to 8 hours, never longer than your permission. The 8 hours run from the device's last contact with Health ID. A full online sign-in is also needed at least every 24 hours.

**Your permission**

At clinics that work offline, the clinician asks for access "for this visit": 1, 4, 8 or 12 hours. You see the length before you approve or decline. You can end access early on the Access and consent page. A device working offline learns this only when it reconnects. Until then, access ends when your permission expires or the 8-hour limit passes, whichever comes first.

**Changes**

Health ID's servers check each change when it arrives. If your permission has ended, the change waits on the device. It is not added to your record, and it is not lost. It is sent once you give access again.

**Who viewed**

Each time staff open part of your record offline, the device logs it. After reconnecting, you see it as "Viewed your record" under "Who viewed or changed your record" on the Access and consent page, perhaps some hours later.

**Protection**

The copy is encrypted and locked with a key made from the staff member's password. After 5 wrong passwords in a row, the copy is erased. No system is completely safe. The encryption protects a locked or stolen device. It does not protect against harmful code running in the browser while the workspace is unlocked.

**Lost devices**

If a device is lost or stolen, staff must tell their facility administrator at once. The copy stays encrypted, and the time limits still apply. Health ID cannot erase a device that stays offline. Health ID's data protection officer decides whether to report it to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission [verify].

**Your rights**

You have rights over your personal data. Ask your facility first. You can also write to support@healthidentitydirectory.com or [DPO name and contact].

**Where**

At first, offline copies run only in a test setup with made-up patients. Real use will start at [first real pilot facility]. Other facilities will be named here later. In effect from [effective date].
